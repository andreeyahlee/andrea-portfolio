E-commerce startup

Pet owners don’t shop. They check, always!

Paw Haus is a new entrant in the e-commerce space. It can’t compete on price with Shopee or Lazada nor with omnichannel pet suppliers, so I designed around the one thing those giants don’t offer. A proof that every product is safe for your specific pet.

Challenge

Paw Haus is a brand-new local store with no brand history and no ability to compete on price. The challenge was building enough trust for a first-time visitor to make a health-sensitive purchase from a store they’d never heard of, in a market where 92% of owners have pets with specialised health needs.

Impact

100% task success on finding safe food, 100% task success on the new owner starter kit, 4.8/5 trust rating in the starter kit task, 96% trust in recommendations. The one miss, a 20% on reordering from which I directly produced an iteration that moved the feature to where users actually expected it.

My Contribution

Solo end-to-end: market research, 12 interviews, card sort study, competitor analysis, IA, ideation, high-fidelity Figma prototype (desktop + mobile), usability testing across 3 tasks, and two rounds of iteration. No team — every decision from research brief to final pixel was yours.

The Brief

A new entrant can’t win a price war.
Trust is the only way to go.

Paw Haus is entering a market with 20-plus competitors, including giants like Shopee, Lazada, and Pet Lovers Centre, with no physical store, no brand history, and no ability to compete on price. Their only edge is selling products the local pet community can actually trust. For this assigned brief, I designed the online store end-to-end, from research and information architecture through to high-fidelity prototype and usability testing.

What the competition gets right and where the gap is

Shopee & Lazada

The Generalists

  • Effortless search and checkout, users know how these work already.
  • Trust from star ratings and review volume, not product detail.
KohePets

The Niche Specialist

  • Predictive search and reliable stock — the clearest product info of any pet store.
  • Gap: no personalisation for a specific pet’s health needs.
Pet Lovers Centre

The Omni-Channel Giant

  • One-click repurchase and a strong loyalty programme keep owners coming back.
  • Gap: no ingredient transparency or allergen filtering.
The Competitive Landscape

Cheap platforms hide ingredients. Specialist stores show them but charge a premium. Paw Haus targets the gap: transparent products at accessible prices.

Research
Interviews

Twelve owners. One shared fear.

I ran interviews with 12 pet owners to understand how they shop and what gets in the way. The finding that drove everything else: 92% (11 of 12) had a pet with a specialised diet or health condition, most commonly a sensitive stomach or allergy. For them, the wrong product doesn’t mean a bad purchase. It means a sick pet.

Research participants snapshot

Four patterns came up in nearly every conversation:

11 of 12

Health first, price second

Ingredients matter more than brand or deal to these owners, health is wealth.

9 of 12

They need outside proof

Vet or community validation is essential, they won’t trust a store they don’t recognise.

10 of 12

They shop with a target

They already know what they want. They’re not browsing — they don’t want to waste time.

7 of 12

Price tips the final decision

Store loyalty is low. A better deal elsewhere is the top reason carts get abandoned.

card sort for IA

How owners actually group products.

To decide how products should be organised on the site, I ran a card sort with 20 products spanning food, health, accessories, and devices, given to a separate pool of 15 participants from the interview pool.

The result was consistent: owners grouped products by pet type and function, not by brand. That single insight became the backbone of the site’s navigation rather than the brand-first structure most competitors use.

Synthesis
Interviews

Two people. Two different fears.

The 12 interviews clustered into three themes: Ingredient Anxiety, Navigation Friction, and Brand Trust. Two personas were created to embody those tensions and letting their situations anchor every design decision.

Affinity mapping

Amy & Teddy - The Anxious Health Advocate

“I actually check the ingredients to make sure there’s not any random stuff I don’t understand. So for food, I 100% always check!”

Design question

How might we let Amy find safe products effortlessly, so she can stop manually vetting every label?

Amy’s dog Teddy was recently diagnosed with a beef allergy. Every purchase now means manually reading every label — exhausting, but non-negotiable. She shops on KohePets and Floof because they at least show ingredient lists, but still manually verifies every product before buying.

She Needs

  • Allergen filtering without manual label-reading
  • A clear signal when a product’s recipe changes
  • Confidence she can trust the platform and not just the individual product
Victor & Princess - The First-Timer Value Seeker

“If they’re young, it’s just spray and pray. You (over) buy things to see what they like. Most of it won’t get used. So I’m not willing to invest in high-quality products, …”

Design question

How might we give Victor a structured starting point, built on community consensus rather than the store’s word?

Victor just brought home Princess, an 8-week-old kitten. He has no idea what a new cat actually needs and trusts community reviews far more than any store recommendation — his fear is wasting money on things she’ll reject.

He Needs

  • Guidance on what to actually buy first
  • Proof from other pet owners — not the store
  • A path that reduces the chance of wasted purchases
The Solution

Four moves that turned browsing into checking.

onboarding

Pet Profile & Smart Filtering

“How might we identify the right products based on a pet’s actual needs?”

On the initial visit, users create a profile for their pet (breed, age, and health conditions like a beef allergy). The entire catalogue is then automatically pre-filtered for that pet. As a returning user, you never have to re-enter your pet’s allergy. Every product listing has already been checked. The underlying structure follows the card sort finding directly: products are organised by pet type and function first, never by brand.

trust at a glance

Transparency & endorsement

“How might we convey a product’s reliability without the user having to dig for it?”

Every product card carries a one-line verdict: “Perfect for Teddy” or “⚠ Not suitable, contains allergen: beef.” The homepage opens with expert endorsements and trust badges. The product page surfaces a full ingredient breakdown and a personalised allergen warning — no guesswork.

restock watchdog

Restock automation & watchdog alerts

“How might we remove Amy’s need to re-check safe products every time she reorders?”

A “Time to Restock” module on the homepage predicts what’s running low based on past orders. Owners set a personalised reminder schedule at checkout. If a product Teddy already eats updates its recipe, a watchdog alert flags it immediately — so a previously-safe staple never quietly becomes a risk.

for first-time pet owners

New pet parent guide & community ratings

“How might we give Victor a structured, community-backed starting point?”

A dedicated “New Pet Parent” page in the nav offers a community-vetted Starter Kit — pre-selected essentials Victor can add to his cart in one go. Across the site, visible star ratings, review counts, and a “Community Pick” badge give him the social proof he trusts more than any store recommendation.
